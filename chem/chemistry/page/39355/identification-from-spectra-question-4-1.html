Identification from spectra - Question 4: Feedback

Which of the following best describes your feedback?

Spectroscopic identification of organic compounds: Question 4

This page on identifying a compound from its spectra can be marked as direct student access either for assigning it as a test or for students to work on in their own time. If you do not wish to use student access, links to downloadable versions of the question and, separately the worked answer, can be found at Printable versions of written tasks.

Identify Compound D and explain how the data from each of (a) to (d) provides the information to support your answer.

(a) Elemental analysis of Compound D

(b) Mass spectrum of Compound D

(c) Infrared spectrum of Compound D

(d) Low resolution 1H NMR data for Compound D

Words: 1-500
Show model answer
(a) From the elemental analysis the empirical formula is C3H7Br
(b) The M+ peaks at m/z = 122 and 124 leads to the conclusion that the molar mass of Compound D is the same as the empirical mass and the molecular formula is C3H7Br. There are two peaks due to the two isotopes of bromine 79Br and 81Br (which are present in roughly equal amounts to give an actual molar mass for Compound D of 123 .01 g mol-1). The fragment at m/z = 43 is due to loss of bromine leaving C3H7+.
(c) The absorption at 2987 cm-1 is due to C–H. Absorptions due to C–Br are not on the syllabus and no other information can be obtained from the infrared spectrum without computer analysis of the fingerprint region.
(d) The 1H NMR spectrum shows that the hydrogen atoms are in two different chemical environments. One of them indicates a single hydrogen atom which suggests –CHBr- and the six other hydrogen atoms suggest that there are two –CH3 groups attached to this central carbon atom. This is confirmed by the relative upfield chemical shift of the signal due to the single hydrogen atom bonded to the carbon atom containing the bromine atom at 4.3 ppm relative to the 1.7 ppm shift of the hydrogen atoms in the two methyl groups.
All this information taken together confirms that Compound D is 2-bromopropane CH3CHBrCH3.
