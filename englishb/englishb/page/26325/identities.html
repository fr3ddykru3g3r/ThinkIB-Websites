Identities

  • /
  • Themes, 2018 onwards/
  • Identities

The concept of 'identity' covers a massive area, from the identity of the individual to the identity of social groups. And anyway, there is the fundamental problem that it is (almost inherently?) subjective ... my notion of a given identity is probably not going to match your notion.

On the other hand, it's a fascinating area to explore - and it's also essential for sanity, since we all navigate through a map of identities.

Identity & language

From the point of view of language, 'identity' is expressed by personal pronouns, fundamentally, and so our sense of identity is made concrete by...

I ... you (single) ... he / she / it ... we ... you (group) ... they (distant group)

And we might sub-divide those into...

I ... you (single) ...  we ... you (group)

= recognised as subjects / addressed personally

versus

he / she / it ... they (distant group)

= recognised as objects / described impersonally

So, the fact that identity forms part of the most basic structure of language indicates its importance in our view of the world - and we may then ask about the extent that such language structures form, or even restrict, our view of identity.

Finally, there's the 'First Person Factor' - reflecting the unavoidable fact that identity begins at home: i.e. inside our own head. We make judgements about identity depending on our own identity, because each of us is the centre of the world (of course). This is also known as the 'ME ME ME' bias, and it makes the grouping of pronouns look like this...

I ... we = familiar identity

versus

you (single) ... he / she / it ... you (group) ... they (distant group)

= unfamiliar identity

Now, we're all familiar with the personal pronouns ... but perhaps less aware of the patterns that they form, as suggested above. Those patterns suggest different species of identity, and therefore different ways of thinking about identity. The core distinction is between the identities that we know (or think we know): 'I' and 'we' - and the identities that we assume we know (but actually only guess): 'he / she / it', 'you' and 'they'. A huge amount of social conflict arises from, I suggest, the prejudices and stereotypes that follow from the identity distinctions between 'us' and 'them'.

Concepts of identity

Cultural diversity ... a theoretical overview of how different (cultural) identities interact - useful for exploring the whole field

History & tradition  ... extract from a novel: a dialogue about how history and tradition are vital for defining a sense of who you are

Class, jokes, and linkers  ... short text about how class structure underlies certain types of British humour - but what do we mean by 'class' ... how do we recognise class differences? ... and what effect does class have on Identity?

Respecting cultural diversity?   ... controversial text about what it means to 'respect' cultural differences - should we?

Case studies

English in India    ... how language helps to define who you are - in many different ways - and how your (group) identity has an effect of defining or changing language

Glimpses of Islam   ... two texts which explore the ways in which identity may be defined by religion, in this case, Islam

Burka Avenger   ... focuses on an animation which deconstructs and re-constructs ideas about the burka ... good for raising issues about how fictions express, and formulate, identities and social norms...

The burka issue   ...  a file of sources in order to explore whether or not there should be rules / laws governing how clothing expresses identity - can we wear what we want?

Criminalising clothes   ... source texts on the controversy about the 'burkini' - why does the way that some people choose to express their identity through clothing cause other people to feel that their identity is threatened ?

Imagining America    ... brilliantly created dialogue by Bill Bryson, which explores the way that identity may be defined by what you think about America

Migrant viewpoints   ... exploration of how identity is influenced by the experience of being an immigrant (in this case, in the US)

Mini-essays ... the stimulus texts give short sketches of two societies (Dutch and Spanish), illustrating the idea of observing rules and norms - and summing them up concisely so as to define 'Identity'

Cosmopolitans    ... how the 'international' or 'transnational' view of the world works

Nationalist myths   ... a lively, accessible account of conversations with nationalists, evoking how they see the world

Race relations ... from Chimamanda Ngozi Adichie's Americanah (2013), a novel which follows the progress of a young Nigerian woman, Ifemelu, who migrates to the US

Tourist tribes ... a sociological description + analysis - i.e. how we observe and define identity ...

Tourists ... a dialogue, suggesting different attitudes of different types of tourist - with different types of identity ...

English identity

The English are best   ... witty, satirical song by Flanders and Swann, sending up the way that the English think they're absolutely The Best - cloze transcript available

Class, jokes, and linkers   ... a brief text suggesting that types of humour can tell us much about the English class system - possibly an elderly text and point of view, but should stimuluate discussion ... excellent for illustrating use of linkers to construct complex arguments

Elitist Britain? ... a range of activities based on an official report analysing hierarchy in British society ... revealing about the particular structure, and the values, of British society

A British source ... an exploration of an accessible online site containing basic information about Britain - good as a starting point for further research

The Xenophobe ... a satiric poem about the disturbing strain of xenophobia in English (UK) society

'Being fair' ... quite complex text which sets out to explain a defining quality of English identity ... good for prompting reflection on the 'defining qualities' of students' own cultures...

On queuing ... a related, and possibly more accessible, text about English identity...

TEA ... an analysis of a packet of tea - proposing that one can extra a great deal about culture and identity by examining closely the common items of a culture ... again, good for prompting student reflection

English Nature ... primarily useful as a demonstration of close reading and analysis, the subject matter of the 'English character' is explored through a complex, and suggestive, metaphor ... could be used to prompt student's own perceptions
