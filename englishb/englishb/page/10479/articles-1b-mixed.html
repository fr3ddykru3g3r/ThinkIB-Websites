Instructions

1. The exercise below contains sentences which have gaps into which you have to insert one of the options a / an / the / no article

2. For each gap, choose the option which fits correctly

3. When you have finished, click on 'Total score: CHECK' at the bottom, and review how you have done

** To learn the most from this test, you should then think carefully about your mistakes - work out why you got each one wrong, and think about the reasons for the right answer.

.

This exercise has examples which include the Generic / Definite / Indefinite distinction in the same sentence. You have to choose which is the correct one for each of the gaps.

.

 

(1) television was in (*) corner of (*) room

We are talking about a particular television and where it is.

 

(*) television was in (2) corner of (*) room

There will be four corners, and there is no information that we are talking about a particular corner - so it is one example of several.

 

(*) television was in (*) corner of (3) room

Given the rest of the sentence, it must mean the particular room where the television is.

 

(4) cats are (*) pain in (*) ass.

There is no indication that we are talking about a particular collection of cats, so the statement is general, and the noun is plural.

 

(*) cats are (5) pain in (*) ass.

No indication that the pain is particular - it is just associated with cats ! So, it is an example of many.

 

(*) cats are (*) pain in (6) ass.

Well, you only have one ass ...

 

(7) neighbours can be difficult, but (*) guy next door is (*) real disaster.

The sentence starts talking about neighbours (plural) in general.

 

(*) neighbours can be difficult, but (8) guy next door is (*) real disaster.

We are obviously talking about a single, particular person, here.

 

(*) neighbours can be difficult, but (*) guy next door is (9) real disaster.

There must be other disasters in the world, so this is one case in many.

 

I do not know much much about (10) cars, but (*) car I saw was (*) Mercedes.

The speaker is obviously talking about the whole general topic of cars (plural).

 

I do not know much much about (*) cars, but (11) car I saw was (*) Mercedes.

Now, we are talking about one particular car - the car that he saw.

 

I do not know much much about (*) cars, but (*) car I saw was (12) Mercedes.

There must be a lot of different types of Mercedes, so we are talking about one example among many.

 

(13) evolution is based on (*) idea that animals need to adapt to (*) environment.

Evolution is the name given to a big general idea.

 

(*) evolution is based on (14) idea that animals need to adapt to (*) environment.

The idea is immediately described, using the clause beginning with that, and so made particular.

 

(*) evolution is based on (*) idea that animals need to adapt to (15) environment.

The 'environment' mentioned is singular, as one example of many that could apply - if it had been plural, it would have required no article. Then, in addition, 'environment' begins with a vowel - so we need 'an'.

 

(16) idea behind (*) reality shows is to show (*) example of real life.

It is a particular idea because we immediately are told that it is behind - in other words, it is not just any general idea.

 

(*) idea behind (17) reality shows is to show (*) example of real life.

The noun is plural, so it is not particular - and it is a generalisation, so no article.

 

(*) idea behind (*) reality shows is to show (18) example of real life.

We are given no information to show that this is a particular example - and the word 'example' begins with a vowel.

 

Diplomacy is (19) art of saying nice doggie! until you can find (*) stone.

This is a particular art because we are told it is the specific "art of saying..."

 

Diplomacy is (*) art of saying nice doggie! until you can find (20) stone.

It's not some particular stone - any stone will do !
